Wati · Phase 3 · Structure

Information architecture

Nineteen screens, every one carrying the job it serves. Four flows with their decisions, their states and both endings. A coverage matrix that leaks in exactly the two places the evidence is thinnest — which is the matrix working, not failing.

2026-08-19 · sources of truth: ia/sitemap.md, ia/flows.md · Research · People

Where the structure comes from. Screens are derived from the jobs in people/jtbd.md, not from any competitor's menu. The primary persona is “the one who has already failed once” — a 2–10 person business that bought a BSP on price and watched it fail — and the MVP core is three jobs: live within the hour, find out about a bad answer before the customer does, and leave without a fight.

Four decisions worth arguing with. (1) Overview lands, not an inbox — the brief calls the inbox “an exception surface, not the workspace”. (2) There is no per-conversation queue for the primary persona at all; Conversation exists only inside a theme, as evidence. (3) Replay is inline, never a destination — the persona's whole setup budget is under 30 minutes. (4) Account is a first-class branch and Export is a peer of Billing, because a buried exit is the defect this product argues against.

Honesty note on the main job's 0. A budget met by declaring the landing screen the answer is usually a budget being gamed, so it is stated rather than claimed. The main job is closed by the AI doing the work, not by a screen. The counts to hold later phases to are the MVP-core ones: R1 at 3, R2 at 1–2, R5 at 2. R1 sitting exactly at the limit is the number to watch — a fourth step breaches this budget and the brief's 10-minute criterion at the same time.

Flows

Flow 1 — Main job: repeat questions handled without me

When the same questions keep arriving on WhatsApp and I am the one answering them, I want them handled without me, so that I can spend my day on the work only I can do.

Endings. 2 successes · 1 dead end — the channel is out of the buyer's hands. The “nobody on duty” dead end was removed at the critique, not by pretending it cannot happen, but by giving it a fallback reply and a signal on Overview.

Flow 2 — R1: live within the hour

When I am setting this up for the first time, I want to see it answering my own real questions within the hour, so that I know I have not bought another thing that does not work.

Endings. 1 success · 1 dead end — the number cannot be migrated because it is bound elsewhere. Approval pending never blocks setup: that branch was the category's failure drawn as if it were ours, and it contradicted the 10-minute criterion.

Flow 3 — R2: find out before the customer tells me

When a customer gets a bad answer, I want to find out before they tell me, so that I am not learning about my own business from a complaint.

Endings. Success, plus a regression loop rather than an ending. No dead end by design — every branch returns to Theme detail or Themes.

Flow 4 — R5: leave without a fight

When it stops being worth it, I want to leave without a fight, so that I am not trapped paying for something I have stopped using.

Endings. Success only. The absence of a dead end is the design — the reviews record unresponsive support after a cancellation request, dark patterns hiding the cancel form, and charges continuing for months.

The first pass had zero orphan jobs, and it was wrong. S1 was ticked against Handover/Inbox/Handled conversation and S2 against What it costs and saves. Reading the rows back out loud, naming the screen for each job, both turned out to be stretches placed to make the matrix look complete. Both ticks were removed.

1 orphan screen · 2 orphan jobs, all resolved, none deleted quietly. People and access — the brief requires it, no job produces it; kept at Deep level with its orphan status visible rather than laundered with an invented job. It becomes a voice problem at phase 5. S2 (“look more capable than our headcount”) — served incidentally by the main job working at all. Both go to the backlog. The matrix leaks in exactly the two places the evidence is thinnest, and those are the same two jobs.
