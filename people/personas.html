Personas

The one who has already failed once Primary

Chosen by the human at the persona gate, against a recommendation for Persona 2 — evidence over inference.

Context

A 2–10 person D2C or services business, most often India. They bought a BSP on price, set it up themselves in an afternoon with a drag-and-drop builder, shipped a flow, and watched it fail to hold a real conversation. Still answering WhatsApp personally while paying ₹1,500–5,000/month plus per-message costs.

Jobs

Get the repeat questions answered without them; stop paying for something that does not work; be able to leave without a fight.

Pains confirmed

The automation is felt to be shallow but they cannot articulate why. Support takes days ("replies took 2-3 days"). And the exit is worse than the product: dark patterns on the cancellation form, forced annual billing at checkout, charges continuing for months.

Convinces them hypothesis

A number they can check themselves rather than a number they are told. Inferred from the market needing to guarantee a rate rather than merely claim one — no reviewer says it.

Scares them off confirmed

Annual lock-in discovered at checkout, and support that disappears exactly when needed. The sharpest evidence in the project: their BSP offers "no technical support available and only a chatbot is offered, with no human support even after sending emails." Their lived experience of AI support is being stonewalled by one.

"Sometimes it feels limited in automation options and not flexible enough for complex workflows"— Sulimam A, Design, 2–10 employees · Capterra

The one who owns the number Secondary

Proposed as primary and overruled at the gate. The argument still stands on its own terms; what it lacked was evidence.

Context

Support or ops lead at a scaling D2C brand, roughly 11–50 people, 5–10 agent seats, WhatsApp volume high enough that cost per conversation is a line item someone asks about. [?] how they came to own the tool is not in the evidence and is deliberately not asserted.

Pains confirmed

They cannot prove the number. The AI they have is a paid add-on bolted onto a keyword-flow core, so the thing they are measured on is an upsell rather than the product. And the tool covers one channel while their customers use several.

Convinces them confirmed

Seeing which conversations the AI resolved and being able to disagree with the label. Only Intercom makes the definition contestable — no charge when the customer asks for a human, and a refund if the conversation is reopened.

"Despite multiple requests—five, to be exact—to delete my data and stop emailing me, Wati.io has continued to flood my inbox with spam."— Aaron B, Co-founder, 51–200 employees · Capterra · labelled off-job: the only review from a company this size, and it is about marketing conduct, not daily use

The MVP core

Three jobs: high for the primary persona and uncovered by the market. Read off the matrix, not argued.

  1. See it working on my own real questions within the hour.
    P1 = 3 · uncovered — vendors advertise "10-minute API approval" against a tested "1–2 business days minimum", and nobody designs the waiting state.
  2. Find out about a bad answer before the customer tells me.
    P1 = 3 · uncovered — named only in the aspirational group; the hard group has no vocabulary for it.
  3. Leave without a fight.
    P1 = 3 · actively the opposite — four verbatim reviews document dark patterns, forced annual billing and charges continuing after cancellation.

Two costs, shown before the gate rather than after. The main job is deliberately not in the core — every competitor claims it, so it is table stakes rather than differentiation. And "a number I can defend" misses the core, despite the entire benchmark dimension being built around it, because it scores 2 for the primary persona and 3 for the secondary one. It re-enters the moment the primary persona flips back.

What we still do not know

The dangerous subset — claims that drive design decisions and rest on [?], ordered by what they would cost if wrong.

  1. R4 scores 1 for the primary persona. Narrowed, not closed: the constraint is the time budget, not the concept. Setup must stay "under 30 minutes" because these buyers are "running a business, not a software project" — so replay survives only if it costs seconds and happens inline. Nobody in the corpus has been observed using a pre-flight at all.
  2. R3 scores 2 for P1, and that 2 is a [?]. That single cell kept the benchmark dimension's own job out of the MVP core. A scope decision resting on a guess with a number attached.
  3. Five channels are justified by vendor-side evidence. WhatsApp is confirmed (78% of Indian SMBs), Instagram is supported generically — email and SMS are unevidenced as inbound channels for this persona and are carried on the brief's authority alone.
  4. "Still answering WhatsApp personally" is a hypothesis carrying the main job.
  5. E1's emotional framing is the designer's, not the user's. The trust failures are confirmed; "wants to check things himself" is a reading of them.

The brief's problem statement has now been contradicted twice. It says this buyer switches because the AI is fake. Phase 2a corrected that once — the AI exists, it is an upsell on a keyword-flow core. This phase went looking specifically for people leaving over bad AI answers and found none. What the reviews record instead: the platform "was not working at all", implementation "entirely left to them with no support", a promised Relationship Manager who never appeared, "$150 but the software never worked" with a refund refused. They leave over absent support, billing traps and a product that did not work — not over answer quality. That belongs in /dsf:change against the signed-off brief, not in a quiet patch here.
